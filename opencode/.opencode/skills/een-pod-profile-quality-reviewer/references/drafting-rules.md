# Drafting rules

Source basis: Partnering Profile Quality Guidelines v1.2, sections 4 and 6.2.

## Global writing rules

- Write clear, concise English for generalists/non-specialists.
- Use full sentences; avoid long sentences and unnecessary lists.
- Explain non-common acronyms on first use.
- Check grammar/spelling and avoid unnecessary capital letters.
- Keep all fields internally consistent.
- Avoid repetition, especially between Title, Short Summary and the first paragraph of Full Description.
- Avoid marketing speak and unsubstantiated claims.
- Write in the **third person**. Do not use `we`, `our`, `you`, `your`.
- Use British English unless the user requests another variant.
- Do not invent facts, metrics, certifications, market claims, TRL, IPR, clients, funding calls or deadlines.

## Anonymity

A profile may be anonymous or identifiable. Treat this as an explicit client/adviser decision.

If anonymous:
- remove company/organisation names, brands and trade names;
- do not expose patent/certificate numbers;
- inspect media/file names for identifiers;
- avoid unnecessary indirect identifiers.

If the client waives anonymity, note that this may reveal strategy or IP and may attract direct approaches.

## Cooperation explanation

For every selected cooperation type, explain:
- **WHY** the cooperation type was selected / desired outcome;
- **HOW** the cooperation is envisaged in practice.

Do not simply repeat a generic partnership label.

## Full Description

Write it as a **stand-alone text**, not as a continuation of the Short Summary.
Give enough detail for a potential partner to decide whether to submit an EoI.

## Evidence discipline

Prefer specific evidence:
- measurable performance;
- quantified advantages;
- relevant standards/certifications only when verified;
- past international experience only when verified;
- comparison with alternatives where supported.

Remove empty claims such as "unique", "best", "high quality", "competitive price" unless evidence explains them.
